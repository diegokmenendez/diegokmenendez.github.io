Research

I currently work in X Energy Lab at UC Irvine under Dr. Xian Shi. I designed, built, and now run our 2 inch Rotating Detonation Engine (RDE). I enjoy this intersection of fluid dynamics, chemistry, and hands on engineering. I have also been interested in working on questions related to the structure of detonations. More info on both endeavors below.

Rotating Detonation Engines

I adapted an Air Force Research Lab RDE design down to a 2 inch scale and designed the facility around it from scratch, including the feed, data acquisition, ignition, and control systems for methane and oxygen at up to 600 g/s. After ten months of work we reached our first hotfire in December 2025. Since then we have run over 200 more hotfires. This RDE serves as a material testing platform for observing failure mechanisms of various materials in the RDE environment and will eventually serve to evaluate new materials, designed specifically to withstand the RDE environment.

Detonation Cellular Structure

I have become more and more interested in the questions of detonation cell size and detonation cell regularity. I think these problems are within our grasp and the right framing would help answer them. I am interested in these questions because they ask how nature chooses certain behaviors over others. So far, I have analyzed hydrogen-oxygen detonations with argon dilution and compared the statistics of their triple point collisions. I hope to continue to look for more ways to understand these questions.
